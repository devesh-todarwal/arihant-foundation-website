What We Believe

A better world is not a dream to wait for. It is a choice — pro-nature, pro-poor and pro-women — made real by the consistent effort of ordinary people.

Arihant Foundation brings together human-rights advocates, social workers, thinkers and the youth to face inequality, poverty and climate change — not with despair, but with solutions.

The Work

Six commitments, one mission

Every initiative of Arihant Foundation is rooted in the same soil — justice, dignity, sustainability and collective action.

01 — Legal Empowerment

Rights that reach people

Awareness drives and access-to-justice work, so that the law protects those it was written for — not just those who can afford it.

02 — Women's Leadership

Women who lead

Championing women and girls as decision-makers — in their homes, their villages and their institutions.

03 — Climate & Environment

A liveable tomorrow

Grassroots climate action — from indigenous seed conservation to environmental justice — for a resilient, sustainable future.

04 — Youth Engagement

Young changemakers

People-centred power

Advocating for accountability and integrity in governance — because strong communities deserve institutions worthy of them.

Celebrating the Badak-wasti Water Initiative
From the Field · Water

The water that reopened the schools

In Badak-wasti, the recent dry years had turned water into the village's hardest daily question. And when a village runs short of water, its schools are among the first places to go quiet.

So the foundation went to the root of the problem and established a borewell water line for the community. The water returned, and the schools reopened with it. Just as importantly, the village now has a dependable way to manage the dry months, on its own terms.

“Water first. Everything else follows.”
Badakwasti School · Community Seed Bank Campaign
From the Field · Flagship Programme

The children who guard the seeds

At a village school in Badakwasti, a group of children recently became custodians of something older than any of us: the indigenous seeds of Indian agriculture.

Through the Arihant Community Seed Bank, they learned to collect, keep and pass on the hardy, nutritious seed varieties their grandparents farmed — seeds that resist drought, feed families, and carry a living heritage in their husks. What they experienced was more than a lesson. It was pride: the pride of becoming young guardians of their own future.

“Every seed we sow carries the hope of a sustainable future.”

From the Field · Education

Evenings where futures are written

Across the city's neighbourhoods and village schools, Arihant's learning circles gather children onto one floor — girls and boys side by side — for lessons, stories, art, and the questions no one else has time to answer.

Some evenings end with hand-painted diyas. Some end with a shared meal. All of them end the same way: with a child who believes, a little more firmly, that their future is theirs to write.

“Education is the greatest leveler — and this generation's greatest edge.”
Our Story

It began at home.

Sangeeta Desarda grew up in a house where service was not an occasion — it was the daily weather. Her father, Professor Hiralal Desarda, was an economist who set aside position and title to devote his life to social welfare: championing farmers, fighting for water conservation, advocating tirelessly across India for the land and the people who live from it. Her mother, Shakuntala, a nutritionist, taught that sustainable food and farming could quietly transform a family's life. Their example cast a deep and lasting impression.

It followed her into the law — through more than a decade of practice at the Aurangabad Bench of the Bombay High Court, where she watched the people who most needed justice arrive least equipped to claim it.

And all the while, one truth kept sharpening: the world was not moving toward its Sustainable Development Goals — it was drifting away from them. A better world, she realised, does not happen on its own. Someone has to bring it about.

So during the COVID years, she committed herself to a single question: what can ordinary people do at the grassroots that ripples into something far bigger? Her answer, in August 2022, was Arihant Foundation — a name that, in an old Indian tradition, belongs to those who conquer their inner enemies. Fear. Apathy. Indifference.

At its heart lies her deepest conviction: education is the greatest leveler — the greatest edge this generation holds. Shared equally between girls and boys, it is how she believes every crisis of sustainability, from inequality to a warming planet, will finally be solved.
